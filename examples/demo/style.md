# Style

House style, calibrated once from `calibration/style-options.md` (picks: 1-A,
2-B minus purple, 3-B, 4-A, 5-C with a little B). `/vn-style` confirms or
adjusts it per project; every writing step loads this file. Every rule is
checkable by reading a scene.

## Narration
- Person & tense: second person ("you"), present tense. Always.
- Rhythm: mostly short sentences. At most one long sentence per paragraph,
  and it earns its length with a concrete detail, not with clauses.
- Sensory and emotional detail is welcome: one concrete sensory detail per
  beat (heat of a cup, a radio somewhere). Never stacked modifiers; at most
  one adjective per noun; at most one simile per scene.
- Interiority: a thought, then the feeling named once, plainly ("That's
  new."). Never a paragraph of inner monologue. Never name a feeling the
  reader already saw.
- Narration never describes the player's own face or body. The player is
  the camera. Their hands may enter frame.
- No chapter or scene titles on screen.
- A narration block is at most two lines; prefer one.

## Dialogue
- Dialogue : narration ratio: about 70:30. When in doubt, cut narration.
- Lines are short enough to be spoken in one breath; split anything longer
  into two `say` steps.
- No honorifics (no -san, -senpai, etc.) and no localisation-flavoured
  speech. Characters sound like people, in plain English.
- Stutters, cut-offs and interjections are written in the text
  (`"H-hi."`, `"I just—"`); they carry to TTS.
- Each character's lines obey their sheet's Voice section; the sheet wins
  over this file.
- No "..." as a whole line. No "I mean," openers.

## Text box limits
- Max characters per line: 180
- Max lines per narration block: 2

## Choices
- Phrasing: what the player would say or do, in their voice, under eight
  words. Never "Option A / Option B" or meta descriptions.
- Every option changes at least one line that follows.

## Tone dials
- Humour: sincere by default. Warmth and the occasional goofy beat are fine
  when a character's sheet allows it; never a joke that undercuts a sincere
  beat, and never a joke per line.
- Melodrama: low. Big feelings arrive in small sentences.
- Explicitness of emotional telling: show, then name it once if it matters.

## Never
- Never describe a character's appearance in narration after their first
  scene, except a change (a new outfit, hair down).
- Never summarise what just happened.
- Never end a scene on a rhetorical question.
