# Style calibration

**House picks (recorded 2026-10-28):** 1-A, 2-B with less purple prose
(sensory and emotional detail stays), 3-B, 4-A, 5-C with a little B. No
chapter titles; narration never describes the player's body; no honorifics;
180-character line limit. These live in `templates/project/style.md`.

`/vn-style` writes a project's `style.md` from the human's picks. This file
defines the axes and carries a standing set of samples so the human can pick
by reading rather than by describing. Samples below share one beat: the
player (a transfer student) meets a girl running a tea stall at night; she
hands over a cup before he can ask his question.

Pick one per axis, and mark any individual line you dislike. Line notes are
worth more than the picks.

## Axis 1 — narration person & tense

**A. Second person, present** (most modern VNs)
> You stop at the stall because it's the only one with stools. She doesn't look up until the cup is already in front of you.
>
> "Drink that first."

**B. First person, past** (classic VN localisations)
> I stopped at the stall because it was the only one with stools. She didn't look up until the cup was already in front of me.
>
> "Drink that first."

**C. First person, present**
> I stop at the stall because it's the only one with stools. She doesn't look up until the cup is already in front of me.
>
> "Drink that first."

## Axis 2 — rhythm

**A. Short, flat**
> The tea is hot. The stools are new. Nobody else is sitting on them.

**B. Mixed, one long sentence per paragraph**
> The tea is hot enough that you hold it by the rim, and the stools are so obviously new that you wonder who she bought them for. Nobody else is sitting on them.

**C. Long, flowing**
> The tea is hot enough that you hold the cup by its rim and wait, and the stools are new in the way things are new when someone has been thinking about them for a long time before buying them, which makes you wonder, a little, who they were for.

## Axis 3 — interiority budget

**A. Lean: one thought per beat, never named**
> She watches you drink. You drink.

**B. Moderate: a thought, then the feeling named once**
> She watches you drink. You drink, and realise you have stopped wanting to ask the question. That's new.

**C. Generous: full inner monologue**
> She watches you drink. You drink, and somewhere in the second mouthful you notice the question has gone, not answered but set down, and you wonder whether that was the tea or her or the fact that nobody has watched you do anything in months.

## Axis 4 — dialogue : narration ratio

**A. Dialogue-heavy (70:30)**
> "You found the street."
> "It was lit."
> "It usually is. Most people walk past it twice."
> "I only walked past once."
> "Then drink that, and we'll see."

**B. Balanced (50:50)**
> "You found the street," she says, and slides the cup across. "Most people walk past it twice."
>
> You turn the cup. It's too hot to drink.
>
> "I only walked past once."
>
> "Then drink that, and we'll see."

**C. Narration-heavy (30:70)**
> "You found the street," she says. She slides the cup across without looking at it, the way people do with things they've done ten thousand times, and the steam comes up between you. You turn the cup. It's too hot to drink, and she knows it, and she waits anyway. "Most people walk past it twice."

## Axis 5 — humour

**A. Dry, under the line**
> "The stools are new."
> "I didn't ask."
> "No. You were going to."

**B. Warm, a little goofy**
> "The stools are new. Don't get excited, I got them for the cat."
> "You have a cat?"
> "No."

**C. None; sincere throughout**
> "The stools are new," she says, and then, quieter, "I wasn't sure anyone would use them."

## Also decide (yes/no each)
- Chapter titles on screen.
- Narration ever describes the player's own face/body (usually no, POV).
- Honorifics or localisation-flavoured speech.
- Hard line length limit for the text box (default 180 characters).
